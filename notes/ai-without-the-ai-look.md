You can usually spot an AI generated website within three seconds. Purple and blue gradient in the background. A rocket emoji next to every heading. Every button, card and input rounded into a pill. A headline that says something like "Revolutionize your digital workflow" without telling you what the product actually does.

I use AI every day. It makes me faster and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But I never let it make the design decisions, because left alone it gives you the average of every template on the internet. Here is how I get the speed without the look.

## Why AI designs all look the same

When you type "build me a modern landing page", the AI has nothing to go on except what it has seen most often. So it gives you exactly that: the most common layout, the most common colours, the most common words. It is not being lazy. You simply did not give it anything better to aim for.

The fix is not a better prompt for the whole page. The fix is to stop asking for whole pages.

## Build one component at a time

Before I build anything, I break the page into pieces: the navigation, the hero, the feature section, the pricing table, the footer. Then for each piece:

1. **Research it first.** I look at how strong real products solved that exact piece. Mobbin shows screenshots from real live apps, which is perfect for mobile screens and flows. Pinterest is surprisingly good if you search for the exact component, like "website footer design inspiration". Dribbble helps with type, spacing and small details.
2. **Pick two or three references** and notice the structure. How many columns? Where does the main button sit? How much space is around the heading?
3. **Give the AI the blueprint.** Instead of "make a footer", I say something like "four column footer, logo and one line about the business on the left, two columns of links, contact details on the right, dark background, left aligned text, no emojis".

The AI goes from guessing to following instructions, and the result looks like it was designed on purpose, because it was.

## The things I remove on sight

- **Gradients everywhere.** When everything is colourful, nothing stands out. I use calm neutral backgrounds and one brand colour, saved for the things you should click.
- **Default fonts.** One well chosen typeface, used with different weights and sizes, looks far more polished than three random ones. This site uses just two.
- **Emojis as icons.** They look different on every phone and computer. A proper icon set looks the same everywhere.
- **Pill shaped everything.** Small tags can be fully round. Big cards should not be.
- **Centred paragraphs.** They are tiring to read. Body text goes on the left.
- **Robot copy.** "Leverage cutting edge synergy" means nothing to a customer. I write the way a person would explain it across a table. If a ten year old would not understand a sentence, I rewrite it.
- **Long dashes in every sentence.** AI loves them. People mostly do not write that way, and readers are starting to notice.

## Design for real life, not the screenshot

AI tends to design only the perfect screen, where every list is full and every form is filled in correctly. Real software starts empty, loads slowly on a weak connection and gets typed into wrongly. So for every screen I also design:

- What it looks like when there is **nothing there yet**, with a clear first step.
- What it looks like **while it loads**, using placeholder shapes instead of one big spinner.
- What happens when something **goes wrong**, with a plain message right next to the problem that keeps what the user already typed.

And I check it on a small phone first. Most visitors will see it there.

## Keep the motion quiet

Animation is like seasoning. A little makes things feel alive. Too much makes people feel sick and makes the product look like a toy. Movement should explain what changed, not show off.

## The short version

Use AI for speed. Keep the taste for yourself. Research each piece, give the AI a clear structure, cut the generic habits and write like a human. That is the difference between a site that looks generated and one that looks built.
